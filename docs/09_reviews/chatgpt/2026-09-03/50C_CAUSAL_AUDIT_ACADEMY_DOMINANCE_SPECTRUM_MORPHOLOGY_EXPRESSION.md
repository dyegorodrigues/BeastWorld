## 2. Locked developmental correction

A Generated Beast is a new person. It matures while its continuity/Core remains protected in the human and may manifest spectral or dense field bodies during development.

At autonomous birth, its definitive biological body reflects that maturation and never resets into infancy. However, a newly autonomous organism may need an adaptation phase:

- independent metabolism must settle;
- strength and tissue resilience may increase;
- new proprioception and balance require practice;
- full-form transition may need progressive stabilization;
- the person remains mentally mature throughout.

Donor Matrix supplies developmental architecture and possibility, never donor soul, personality, memory or reincarnation.

## 6. Candidate A — causal skeleton v0.1

This is a mechanics test, not prose and not approved staging.

### Normal activity

A small advanced class practices a declared two-option defense:

- **recall:** the developing Beast returns toward the human;
- **intercept:** the Beast remains external and blocks the strike.

The training projectile is visible, controlled, nonlethal under normal execution and immediately cancellable by the instructor. Cadet and Beast must choose the same response before a marked decision line.

### Trigger

The cadet sees the projectile angle changing toward the group and chooses recall. The developing Beast sees the same change and chooses intercept to protect the cadet and nearby students.

Both decisions are competent and protective. The failure is timing: they commit on the same instant without confirmation.

### Exact failure

The shared interface opens return and outward motor flow at once.

The manifested body does not become evil or insane. It loses its axis during densification:

- its torso is pulled back toward the cadet;
- one already-dense wing/forelimb continues accelerating outward;
- feedback hits the cadet's nervous system;
- the dense limb is now on course to crush two nearby students against the training wall.

The instructor cancels the projectile immediately. The projectile is no longer the danger. Momentum in the misaligned dense limb is.

### Visible rescue

1. The Director perceives the failure before the limb lands.
2. From outside physical reach, he releases a narrowly timed pulse of Dominance.
3. The developing Beast's bestial motor command freezes; it stops adding force to the limb.
4. The limb still carries momentum. Dominance does not erase physics.
5. The Phoenix, already beside the exercise lane as observer, steps into the trajectory and catches/deflects the dense limb with her humanoid body plus a brief field brace.
6. The impact cracks the floor or barrier, making the remaining force visible.
7. The instructor shuts down the exercise feed and clears the lane.
8. The cadet and developing Beast are taken for medical/resonant assessment. Any later resynchronization happens during care, not through an unexplained gesture in the rescue.

The Director prevents new acceleration. The Phoenix defeats existing momentum. Both actions are necessary and readable.

### Stakes and aftermath

- Without intervention, two nearby cadets could be crushed and the central pair could suffer severe interface/Core injury.
- The minimal broad leakage of Dominance startles other manifested Beasts; it does not nearly kill the whole room.
- The Director is not treated as guilty for saving lives.
- The event reveals a strategic vulnerability: one person's Dominance can involuntarily interrupt allied bestial action.
- The professional consequence is a safety review: lane isolation, emergency barriers, command protocol and graduated exposure training.
- The Phoenix's authority appears through technical judgment and action, not an accusation speech.

## 7. Dominance — intensity spectrum

| Degree | Likely effect | Narrative use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — presence | attention turns; threat/relevance is felt | entrance, social pressure, reading hierarchy |
| 2 — pressure | breath/posture change; fear, caution, hesitation | opening or warning |
| 3 — interruption | involuntary freeze or motor-command break | emergency/tactical window |
| 4 — suppression | collapse, inability to act, unstable field failure | high-power domination with collateral risk |
| 5 — overload | physiological injury, shattered manifestation, possible death | exceptional/terrifying use; never routine |

Boundary: Dominance can overwhelm the body and ability to act. It does not manufacture belief, love, desire, loyalty or consent.

Scientific analogy only: defensive freezing, tonic immobility and later collapse are distinct states. BeastWorld uses that distinction as a ladder and amplifies it through bestial Resonance.
