## 1. Dominance as a temporary defensive interruption

Research on defensive freezing describes it as reduced movement and a temporary brake in anticipation of threat, preparing later action rather than permanently removing agency.

Useful BeastWorld extrapolation:

`bestial salience → involuntary orientation/fear → hesitation/freeze → at higher intensity suppression/collapse`.

Research distinguishes defensive freezing from tonic immobility and collapse-like states. BeastWorld may use this as an intensity ladder. At overwhelming power, Dominance can suppress action completely, destabilize a manifestation, injure or kill. The boundary is not “adults always remain mobile”; the boundary is that Dominance does not rewrite love, belief, loyalty, desire or personality.

## 2. Animatic timing and FPS

Professional animation commonly uses a 24 fps project rate. That does not require a unique drawing for every frame. Drawing “on twos” exposes each drawing for two frames, producing about 12 unique drawings per second. Storyboard animatics may hold a panel much longer and add only camera motion, impact frames or a few key poses.

## 3. Current AI-video production reality

Current generative-video tools can create short image-to-video or multimodal clips, but consistency improves when the production first creates character plates across angles, outfits and framings. Runway explicitly recommends character plates because one reference angle can lead to identity variation.

Relevant current capabilities documented by Runway include:

- short generated clips rather than one reliable long fight;
- input/reference images;
- 24 fps output in the documented Gen-4 workflow;
- current model routes for text-to-video, image-to-video, keyframes and video-to-video;
- Seedance 2.0 within Runway supporting 5–15 second multimodal generations and multiple reference inputs.

Sources:
- https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/26871350018835-How-to-create-longer-videos-and-films
- https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video
- https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/50488490233363-Creating-with-Seedance-2-0

Production conclusion: AI is currently suitable for high-quality previsualization and isolated polished shots, but a coherent broadcast-quality fight still requires shot planning, reference control, editing and correction.

## 5. Solo Leveling / frame-rate clarification

The final container rate and unique image count are different. A sequence can be delivered around 24 fps while holding drawings for multiple frames. Published producer reporting described Solo Leveling Episode 24 at about 17,000 production frames versus roughly 8,000–10,000 for many ordinary episodes, indicating concentrated workload and detail rather than a constant “24 new drawings every second” rule.

## 6. Current 2026 AI-video tools to benchmark later

- Google Flow / Veo 3.1: native audio, reference-driven consistency and extended/multi-shot controls.
- Runway Gen-4.5: 24/25 fps output, complex sequenced prompting and short 2–10 second shots.
- Runway References/character plates: current official route for reducing identity drift.
- Runway Agent 2.0 / Seedance 2.0: multimodal and multi-shot experimentation.

These capabilities justify professional previsualization tests. They do not guarantee broadcast-quality chained combat without shot design, editing and correction.
