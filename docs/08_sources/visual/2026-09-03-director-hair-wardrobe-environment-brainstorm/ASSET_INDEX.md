## Positive extraction

### Image 05 — leading pole

- long loose hair with predatory elegance;
- strong black segmented armor without engulfing the body;
- readable shoulders, torso and sword line;
- volcanic/army context supports scale and institutional presence;
- asymmetry and selective arm exposure can communicate agility and confidence.

This is a mood/silhouette leader, not an approved final costume.

### Images 08 and 10 — state and styling vocabulary

- long hair can be loose, tied or braided without changing character identity;
- exposed-arm combat silhouettes can remain martial if protection and movement justify them;
- visual modes can distinguish training, council, command and battle;
- glowing fissures work best as a temporary activated state, not permanent videogame neon.

### Images 03, 04 and 06 — continuity tests

- useful for checking whether one face/body survives haircut, office, battlefield and environment changes;
- prove value of a visual chronology rather than a static character sheet;
- classical architecture and cloned legions are reference placeholders, not BeastWorld design.

## Explicit anti-reference extraction

From image 07 and the author's comments:

- reject the giant travel overcoat that swallows anatomy and movement;
- reject bulky golden ceremonial armor that expresses authority only through volume and gold;
- reject directly copied Roman/Spartan/Ares iconography;
- reject heavy game-skin presentation and labels as final worldbuilding;
- reject armor that hides the character's body language without functional reason.

Image 09's crest/laurel may inspire a strong command silhouette, but its literal Greco-Roman language is too direct.

## Updated visual thesis

The useful target is not a collage of Rome, ronin and generic dark fantasy. It is:

> **beautiful anime-grade martial fantasy with institutional weight, predatory bodily elegance, functional weapons movement and a civilization-specific material language.**

The Director's continuity should prioritize long hair. Short hair remains a meaningful phase or institutional choice, not a constantly changing gimmick. Styling—loose, tied, braided, partially bound—can change with mission, ceremony, training and intimacy.

## Open visual work

- define BeastWorld-specific armor joints, textiles, fasteners and resonant seams;
- derive architecture from climate, creatures, transport, Full-Form clearances and political institutions;
- create House variation without elemental cosplay;
- design weapons around real drawing, reach, scabbard placement and transformation compatibility;
- produce a clean Director turnaround only after the visual grammar is selected.

Nothing in this manifest approves a final design.
