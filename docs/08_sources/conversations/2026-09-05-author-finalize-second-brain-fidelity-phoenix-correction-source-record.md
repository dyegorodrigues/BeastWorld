## Author concern
The author's principal concern is semantic drift across conversations. Repeated reorganizations have previously produced documentation that was technically orderly but later felt unlike what the author had actually meant or visualized.

The Second Brain therefore must preserve not only conclusions but also:

- the author's mental picture and intended experience;
- explanatory nuance and causal reasoning;
- insights recovered during later re-reads of the brainstorm;
- distinctions that prevent a superficially similar but semantically different reconstruction;
- corrections to speech-to-text or transcription errors;
- rejected/superseded directions and the reason they no longer lead;
- strong live possibilities that are intentionally not promoted;
- the relationship between raw source, later interpretation, decision and current wiki statement.

## Cross-conversation requirement
A new conversation must be able to rehydrate from GitHub/Notion without depending on hidden chat memory and without reopening old V3/V4 material as current truth.

The recovery system must explicitly tell a new agent:

1. what is adopted;
2. what remains open;
3. what was superseded/rejected;
4. what the author was trying to make the world *feel like*;
5. which later audits discovered nuances absent from the short causal summary;
6. where the raw source lives when wording or interpretation is disputed;
7. which known transcription corrections must be applied.

## Non-limitation rule
When preserving author intent, do not reduce the record only to examples the author happened to mention in one message. Examples illustrate the design space; they do not automatically exhaust it.

## Safety against compression
A summary is a router, never a substitute for source. When a detail matters to a creative or structural decision, the new conversation should load the relevant semantic source or verbatim source rather than guessing from a compressed dashboard sentence.
