# BeastWorld — Narrative Planning Scales

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** `GOVERNANCE / CREATIVE PROCESS / NO LORE`

## Purpose
Prevent the project from trying to design the entire saga, every book and every chapter at the same time.

## Scale 1 — Saga vector
Know the major direction, not every event.

Current kinds of questions:
- what promise does the youth phase make?
- what changes when the protagonist becomes an apex adult/Director?
- how does the interworld conflict escalate?
- which relationships/institutions must survive long enough to matter later?
- what mysteries need room rather than early answers?

A saga vector is a **lighthouse, not a railroad**.

Do not lock total book count here.

## Scale 2 — Book spine
Each book needs its own complete dramatic organism:
- opening state;
- inciting incident;
- active dramatic question;
- escalation;
- reversals;
- climax;
- irreversible end state;
- unresolved promises carried forward.

Book 1 must be satisfying even if later books become more spectacular.

## Scale 3 — Sequence map
A sequence is a causal dramatic unit larger than a scene and smaller than a whole book.

Examples:
- Academy arrival/social collision;
- investigation of a Fissure anomaly;
- family/political confrontation;
- training problem with a concrete consequence;
- expedition;
- research aftermath;
- tournament segment;
- enemy operation;
- quiet relationship interval.

Sequences are where action, ordinary life, romance, humor, research, politics and world discovery are braided.

## Scale 4 — Scene
A scene has viewpoint, desire, resistance and change.

Use `CREATIVE_SCENE_GATE.md`.

## Scale 5 — Chapter packaging
Chapters are editorial containers assembled from approved scenes/sequences according to rhythm, viewpoint, suspense and reading experience.

Do not decide chapter count before sequence architecture exists.

A chapter may contain:
- one intense scene;
- several short connected scenes;
- part of a larger sequence;
- a deliberate quiet interval.

## Series/book-count rule
Do not lock `trilogy`, `five books`, `seven books` or another count because the number sounds marketable.

After Book 1 architecture and the youth→adult bridge are visible, compare candidate series shapes by:
- how much essential development each preserves;
- whether time skips erase material that should be dramatized;
- whether each book has its own identity/climax;
- whether escalation remains meaningful;
- adaptation potential for seasons/films/animation without writing the novel as a screenplay.

## Revision rule
When a future insight appears, ask first:

> Does this require changing the current causal spine, or can it live as a future vector?

Do not rewrite Book 1 merely because a cool Book 4 possibility appeared.

Promote backward changes only when the future insight exposes a real contradiction or creates a dramatically superior causal chain worth the cost.
