# BeastWorld — Story-Ready Author Room Workflow

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** `GOVERNANCE / CREATIVE PROCESS / NO LORE PROMOTION`

## 1. Human-facing rule

The author should experience BeastWorld development as a **creative room**, not as a governance system.

Backstage terms such as P0, pass numbers, graph maintenance and provenance remain operational tools. The visible creative conversation should instead answer four simple questions:

1. **How does this world work?**
2. **Who lives inside it and what do they want?**
3. **What happens first that cannot be taken back?**
4. **How does that first story grow into the larger saga?**

## 2. Story-Ready Skeleton

Before chapter prose, BeastWorld needs a minimal interoperable skeleton. It does **not** need every future mechanic, name, region, entity or historical event solved.

### A. Power grammar
Enough must be known to write intelligent conflict:

- what Quintessence is functionally capable of doing in story terms;
- how Blood/Matrix lets a person interact with it;
- what is inherited versus learned;
- how Bestas/Bestas Geradas/Concessão relate to personal capability;
- what a trained fighter can intentionally do;
- costs, limits and counterplay;
- how growth becomes visible without videogame UI.

Target: a few deep generative laws, not a taxonomy of every possible technique.

### B. Social grammar
Enough must be known to understand status, conflict and belonging:

- what exactly a Dynasty is;
- whether House, lineage, political state, territory, military order and ecological stewardship are the same institution or distinct layers;
- how commoners/talents enter power structures;
- what the Academy answers to and what it exists to do;
- only then: naming conventions, titles and proper names.

### C. Character engine
Minimum cast before plot locking:

- protagonist at ~15: desire, strength, blind spot, limitation and relational wound/need;
- protagonist–entity relationship;
- cousin as legitimate formidable successor with independent desire;
- a small peer ensemble with non-overlapping competencies and wants;
- adults with agendas that do not collapse into mentor exposition;
- one concrete enemy face/agent/constraint.

Character creation must include **relationships that change behavior**, not only powers and biography.

### D. Book 1 engine
Before scenes, define:

- opening situation;
- inciting incident;
- active question driving the book;
- escalation chain;
- major reversal;
- coordinated Fissure campaign / Direct Concession placement;
- climax;
- irreversible ending state;
- what Book 1 deliberately does NOT resolve.

### E. Saga bridge
Only after Book 1 has shape decide what the time jump preserves or erases.

Keep at least these models open until tested:

1. extended youth progression before adult transition;
2. Book 1 youth → large Book 2 time jump;
3. phased aging with selective jumps;
4. optional future-fragment/cold-open model if it increases promise without destroying uncertainty.

The adult Director is a destination to be earned, not a mandatory opening configuration.

## 3. The round system

Each major creative question is handled in an **Author Room Round**.

### Step 1 — Research only where it earns its keep
Load only the relevant world cluster and relevant reference research. Search externally when a specific craft/system/story problem benefits from comparison.

### Step 2 — Generate genuinely different models
Normally present **3 structurally distinct options** (occasionally 2 or 4 when that is more honest).

Each option should include:

- the felt image;
- how it works;
- why it helps BeastWorld;
- what it forces elsewhere;
- main risk;
- reference lessons used;
- resemblance/copying hazard;
- status (`HYPOTHESIS` until accepted).

The author may choose one, reject all or hybridize pieces.

### Step 3 — Stress-test
Test the preferred model against:

- current Working Canon;
- character agency;
- ensemble relevance;
- scene yield;
- counterplay and competence;
- world/history consequences;
- future scaling;
- originality/resonance risk;
- complexity budget.

### Step 4 — Author reaction is data
The author does not need to answer in formal vocabulary. Reactions such as “this feels right,” “this is sterile,” “this is generic,” or “keep this piece but not that one” are treated as design evidence.

### Step 5 — Promote only what was actually chosen
After explicit acceptance:

- record source;
- update decision/status;
- update current Wiki/article;
- update relevant graph/relations;
- preserve rejected alternatives with reason;
- refresh current routing only when the milestone materially changes it.

Do not create a new bureaucratic layer for every conversational sentence.

## 4. Recommended development order

The process is **braided**, not rigidly linear. Story questions are allowed to expose missing mechanics, and mechanics are solved only far enough to support story.

### Round 1 — Power & combat skeleton
Build 3 candidate grammars for how Quintessence → Blood/Matrix → learned martial expression → Beast relationship → Concession fit together, including costs and growth.

### Round 2 — Society & naming architecture
Decide what Dynasty/House/lineage/state/order mean. Then generate naming families and titles from function, history, language and geography rather than from generic elemental labels.

### Round 3 — Protagonist + relationship web
Define the teenage protagonist and 6–10 high-value relationships/characters, with an ecology of competencies so even an eventual apex protagonist does not make the cast obsolete.

### Round 4 — Book 1 architectures
Generate 3 complete story spines, not isolated opening scenes. Each must use the same current macro-premise but produce a meaningfully different reader experience.

### Round 5 — Select and fuse
Hybridize the strongest architecture, then lock the four current scene gates: incident, protagonist–entity relationship, enemy constraint and minimum ensemble.

### Round 6 — Youth → adult bridge
Only now compare the time-skip models and decide whether Book 2 opens older, whether Directorhood is immediate or later, and what must be dramatized rather than skipped.

### Round 7 — Sequence map
Map chapters/sequences, subplots, reveals, quiet intervals, Academy life, relationships, investigations, travel, research and fights.

### Round 8 — Scene creation
Use `CREATIVE_SCENE_GATE.md`. Draft only after dramatic purpose, viewpoint, desire, resistance, competence and irreversible change are clear.

## 5. Complexity budget

Use the Pass 59 scene-yield test:

> What can this rule make a character do, choose, discover, fear, misunderstand, lose, build, fight, teach or change that the story could not do as well without it?

If the answer is weak, the rule remains open or is simplified.

Prefer:

**few deep rules → many personal/civilizational consequences**

over

**many micro-rules → no story yet**.

## 6. Reference-routing rule

Use Pass 59 WARM when needed.

Examples of reference functions:

- **Jujutsu Kaisen:** individualized power identity, relationships, institution pressure, strongest-character strategic gravity, ensemble consequences;
- **Fog Hill of Five Elements:** body/weapon/personality/terrain/action readability;
- **Solo Leveling:** visible progression and earned presence, with ensemble-depth caution;
- **Fullmetal Alchemist:** few rules generating broad personal/civilizational consequence chains;
- **Hunter x Hunter:** conditions, information and matchup intelligence;
- **World Trigger:** specialist teams and positional competence;
- **One-Punch Man:** how an apex character can still have narrative problems beyond winning fights.

Study function, never import package.

## 7. Current practical definition of “ready to start writing”

BeastWorld is story-ready when:

- a reader-facing power grammar exists;
- a writer-room combat grammar exists;
- social nomenclature has clear meanings;
- protagonist and minimum ensemble have independent wants;
- Book 1 has a causal spine and irreversible end state;
- the first antagonist/constraint is concrete;
- the protagonist–entity relationship can create choices, not only lore;
- the first 3–5 sequences can be described without inventing new foundational rules mid-scene.

Everything else may continue evolving during writing under controlled status.
